Core Principles

Discuss & Tailor: Senior clinician & radiologist discussion is vital. Tailor imaging to the individual child.

ALARP Principle: Increased radiation risk in children. Adhere strictly to 'As Low As Reasonably Practicable'.

CT is Not Mandatory: CT is helpful but should be used judiciously, not as a default for all trauma.

Avoid FAST/Unenhanced US: These can provide false reassurance in blunt abdominal trauma.

CT Exclusion Tool for Blunt Polytrauma (<16s)

Consider NOT scanning an anatomical region if it satisfies ALL criteria below. Senior clinical discretion is paramount.

1. Head

  • GCS > 13 (and not intubated)
  • Does NOT trigger NICE head injury guidelines for CT

2. Neck

  • GCS > 13 (and not intubated)
  • Does NOT trigger NICE C-spine guidelines

3. Chest

  • Chest X-ray normal
  • Chest exam normal
  • NOT rapid deceleration (fall >6m or MVC >40mph)

4. Abdomen

  • No lap belt or handlebar injury
  • No abdominal wall ecchymosis
  • No abdominal tenderness (in conscious pt)
  • No abdominal distension
  • No persistent hypovolaemia (e.g. unexplained tachycardia)
  • No blood from urethra, rectum, or NGT

5. Spine

  • No midline tenderness
  • Low concern on mechanism
  • No neurology (Consider MRI first line if signs present)

Lower Threshold to CT if:

  • Patient needs emergency operation
  • Long secondary transfer required
  • Patient will be sedated in critical care for an extended period
  • Suspected Physical Abuse (SPA) - follow specific RCR guidance
